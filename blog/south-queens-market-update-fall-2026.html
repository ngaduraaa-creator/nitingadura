South Queens Prices Held at $825K in Q3 as Rates Passed 7% — Fall 2026 Update

South Queens market update · Ozone Park, Richmond Hill, South Ozone Park & Woodhaven. By Nitin Gadura, NYS Salesperson #10401383405. Published October 21, 2026.

The short version: 201 one- to three-family homes closed in ZIPs 11416–11421 from July through September 2026, at a $825,000 median. That is up 1.2% from $815,000 in April–June, on about 9% fewer sales. Prices held even as the 30-year mortgage rate climbed to 7.40%, its level for the weeks of October 8 and 15. For sellers, that means demand is still there, but buyers are more payment-sensitive, so pricing right from the first week matters more than timing.
Data: NYC Department of Finance Rolling Sales for Queens (October 2025–September 2026 file), one-, two- and three-family dwellings (building class categories 01–03), sales of $150,000 or more to screen out likely non-arm's-length transfers, recorded closing dates. Two-family homes remain the core of this corridor, about half of all closings, and their median barely moved. Three-family sales were too few (9) to read much into. The ZIP medians swing both ways: Richmond Hill and 11417 rose, while 11416, South Ozone Park and Woodhaven slipped. That is normal at these sample sizes, and the corridor as a whole was flat to slightly up.

Rates are the headwind

Freddie Mac's weekly 30-year fixed average went from 7.03% on September 24 to 7.28% on October 1 and 7.40% on October 8 and 15. On an $825,000 purchase with 20% down, each half-point of rate adds roughly $220 a month to principal and interest. Some buyers stretch and some step back. The next Freddie Mac reading is Thursday, October 22, and the Federal Reserve's next meeting ends October 28.

The borough picture

Across Queens, Compass counted 4,446 deals in Q3 2026, up 1.2% from a year earlier. House closings rose 8.7%, and the biggest gains were in northeastern Queens: Bayside, Auburndale, Flushing and Glen Oaks (Brick Underground). South Queens held price but did not share in that volume gain this quarter.

One thing to keep watching

Queens had 617 pre-foreclosure (lis pendens) filings in Q2 2026, up 17% from a year earlier, according to PropertyShark. A separate 2026 index from NestCash ranked Queens second of 60 New York counties for foreclosure risk, driven largely by mortgage cost burden. PropertyShark's Q3 report has not been released yet. If you are falling behind on payments, talk to a HUD-approved housing counselor early. A planned sale almost always leaves an owner with more than a distressed one.

What this means if you're thinking about selling

Prices in this corridor held through a summer of rising rates, so there's no sign of a falling market. But with fewer sales and buyers watching their monthly payment, an overpriced listing sits. The homes that move are priced to recent closings on the same type of property: a two-family against two-families, not against the ZIP-wide median. If you own a two-family with legal units and separate utilities, you are selling the product this market buys most.
