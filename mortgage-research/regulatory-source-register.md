## 1. TILA / Regulation Z — Advertising Trigger Terms
- **Citation:** 12 CFR § 1026.24 (Truth in Lending Act, Regulation Z, "Advertising")
- **Jurisdiction:** Federal
- **Summary:** Governs mortgage advertisements. The base rule bars stating credit terms that aren't actually available. Subsection (d) defines "trigger terms" — a specific down payment, number of payments/repayment period, payment amount, or finance charge amount — any of which require additional "clear and conspicuous" disclosure: down payment amount/percentage, repayment terms, and the APR (spelled out or abbreviated), plus a variable-rate warning if applicable. A bare interest rate (not APR) stated as a trigger term generally also requires APR disclosure.
- **Source:** https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1026/subpart-C/section-1026.24 · https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1026/24/ · https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/1026.24
- **Verification note:** Full eCFR text not directly fetched in this pass (network-blocked); built from search excerpts of the regulation and CFPB's own page. Pull the full text plus Supplement I official interpretations before final sign-off.

## 2. RESPA / Regulation X — Section 8 Anti-Kickback & AfBA Disclosure
- **Citation:** 12 U.S.C. § 2607 (RESPA §8); 12 CFR § 1024.15 (Regulation X, Affiliated Business Arrangements); related prohibition at 12 CFR § 1024.14.
- **Jurisdiction:** Federal
- **Summary:** §8(a) bars fees/kickbacks/things of value exchanged pursuant to an agreement to refer settlement-service business on a federally related mortgage loan. §8(b) separately bars splitting charges except for services actually performed. §1024.15 creates a conditional AfBA safe harbor: no violation if (1) a written disclosure in the Appendix D format is given to each referred consumer describing the relationship and estimated charges, (2) the consumer isn't required to use the affiliate, and (3) the referrer receives no compensation beyond a permissible ownership return.
- **Source:** https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1024/15/ · https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1024/subpart-B/section-1024.15
- **Direct relevance:** This is the exact provision underlying the AfBA questions already sent to Vineet in the Project CELL brief (any Nitin↔Vineet cross-referral relationship needs the Appendix D-format disclosure, optional-use language, and no referral-fee payment).

## 3. ECOA / Regulation B — Non-Discrimination & Adverse Action Notices
- **Citation:** 12 CFR Part 1002 (Regulation B); notice requirements at §1002.9; statutory basis 15 U.S.C. §1691.
- **Jurisdiction:** Federal
- **Summary:** Bars discrimination on a prohibited basis (race, color, religion, national origin, sex, marital status, age, public-assistance income, or good-faith exercise of CCPA rights) in any aspect of a credit transaction. §1002.9 requires written notice of action taken within set windows (generally 30 days post-completed-application). Adverse action notices need the action taken, creditor identity, an ECOA rights statement, the relevant federal enforcement agency, and either specific reasons or the right to request them. CFPB Circulars 2022-03 and 2023-03 make clear generic/checklist or "black-box model" reasons are insufficient.
- **Source:** https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1002/ · https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1002/9/
- **NEEDS LEGAL REVIEW:** A reported April 2026 CFPB final rule amending Reg B (disparate-impact liability / Special Purpose Credit Programs, per secondary reporting) could not be confirmed against a primary source in this pass.

## 4. Fair Housing Act — Applied to Mortgage Lending
- **Citation:** 42 U.S.C. §§ 3601–3619; implementing HUD regulations at 24 CFR Part 100.
- **Jurisdiction:** Federal
- **Summary:** Bars discrimination in residential real-estate-related transactions — including making, purchasing, or setting terms of a mortgage loan — on race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status, or disability. Applies explicitly to lenders, not just housing providers/brokers; covers denial, discriminatory terms, and discouraging an application. Enforced by HUD (24 CFR Part 100) and DOJ; substantially overlaps ECOA/Reg B.
- **Source:** https://www.justice.gov/crt/fair-housing-act-1 · https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/3601

## 5. SAFE Act — NMLS ID Display on Advertising
- **Citation:** SAFE Mortgage Licensing Act (federal baseline, 12 U.S.C. §5101 et seq.); 12 CFR Part 1007 (Reg G, depository-institution MLOs) §1007.105; 12 CFR Part 1008 (Reg H, minimum state-licensing standards). Gadura, as a state-licensed non-depository broker/banker, is governed by NY state law here, not Reg G.
- **Jurisdiction:** Federal baseline + NY State (operative)
- **Summary:** Federal Reg G requires depository-institution MLOs to make their unique identifier available to consumers on request and in initial written communications — but does not clearly require printing it on every general ad, and doesn't directly govern Gadura anyway. NY's own SAFE Act implementation (Item 8 below) is the operative advertising rule.
- **Source:** https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1007/105/ · https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1007/section-1007.105
- **NEEDS LEGAL REVIEW (flagged, load-bearing):** No single authoritative source was found for the exact required NMLS ID display format/placement (individual MLO ID + company ID, font size, placement) for a NY state-licensed entity. This is squarely the kind of fact the brief says must never be guessed — pull the NMLS "Required Use of NMLS ID" guidance directly and cross-check against current DFS guidance before any ad template is built.

## 6. UDAAP — Mortgage Advertising (Rate Framing, Guaranteed Approval, Urgency Claims)
- **Citation:** Dodd-Frank Act §§1031, 1036 (12 U.S.C. §§5531, 5536 — general UDAAP standard); Mortgage Acts and Practices–Advertising Rule / Regulation N, 12 CFR Part 1014 (more specific and directly on point).
- **Jurisdiction:** Federal (CFPB and FTC share Reg N enforcement)
- **Summary:** Regulation N bars material misrepresentations in commercial communications about mortgage credit products — rates/APR, fees, down payment requirements, and eligibility/qualification claims (e.g., implying anyone qualifies) — and requires 24 months' record retention of related advertising. General UDAAP authority separately reaches unsubstantiated "as low as" claims, false guaranteed-approval claims, or manufactured urgency/scarcity messaging even outside Reg N's specific list.
- **Source:** https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1014 · https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2011/12/16/2011-31731/mortgage-acts-and-practices-advertising-regulation-n-mortgage-assistance-relief-services-regulation
- **NEEDS LEGAL REVIEW:** Secondary commentary describes a 2025–2026 pull-back in CFPB enforcement activity and possible rulemaking narrowing the UDAAP standard; no specific recent (2025–2026) enforcement action naming "as low as," guaranteed-approval, or urgency/scarcity tactics in mortgage ads specifically was independently verified. Treat the "enforcement pull-back" characterization as directional, not confirmed policy.

## 7. TCPA / CAN-SPAM / E-SIGN — Outreach, Email, E-Signature Workflows
- **Jurisdiction:** Federal (all three)

**(a) TCPA** — 47 U.S.C. §227; 47 CFR §64.1200. Restricts autodialed/prerecorded calls and texts and telemarketing calls without prior express consent (written, for marketing). A reported FCC "1:1 consent" rule (2026) would require consent to a single named seller, ending blanket lead-generator consent; also requires DNC-registry honoring, calling-hour limits (~8am–9pm local), and 10-business-day STOP compliance for texts. Statutory damages $500/violation, up to $1,500 if willful.
- **Source:** fcc.gov (general); secondary: https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/insights-finance-cfs-yir-telephone-consumer-protection-act
- **NEEDS LEGAL REVIEW:** Exact effective date/final text of the 2026 "1:1 consent" rule not confirmed from a primary FCC source.

**(b) CAN-SPAM** — 15 U.S.C. §§7701–7713; FTC Rule at 16 CFR Part 316. Requires accurate headers, non-deceptive subject lines, clear ad identification, a valid physical postal address, and a free opt-out honored within 10 business days; applies to B2B email too.
- **Source:** https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business
- **NEEDS LEGAL REVIEW:** Per-violation penalty figure (cited around $53,088) is inflation-adjusted periodically — confirm the current figure against FTC's penalty schedule before citing it anywhere.

**(c) E-SIGN Act** — 15 U.S.C. §7001 et seq. Electronic signatures/records can't be denied legal effect solely for being electronic. For disclosures otherwise required in writing (TILA/RESPA disclosures included), the creditor must obtain the consumer's affirmative, informed consent to e-delivery, including demonstrating the consumer can access the e-form used, before substituting e-disclosure for paper.
- **Source:** https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/7001

## 8. NY State — DFS Regulation of Mortgage Bankers/Brokers & Advertising Rules
- **Citation:** NY Banking Law Article 12-D (§§590 et seq.; mortgage banker licensing at §591, mortgage broker registration at §591-a); advertising-specific rules at 3 NYCRR Part 38, specifically §38.2 ("Solicitation and advertising").
- **Jurisdiction:** NY State
- **Summary:** Article 12-D requires mortgage bankers to be licensed and mortgage brokers to be registered with NY DFS (successor to the former NYS Banking Department post-2011 merger), and governs standards and grounds for suspension/revocation. Part 38 (confirmed current/in force via Cornell LII and Justia as of this search) separately governs advertising: §38.2 requires (1) the legend "Registered Mortgage Broker–NYS Banking Department" or "Licensed Mortgage Banker–NYS Banking Department" (or words to like effect) on ads, (2) the entity's name and a NY street address of at least one office (waived for entities with more than 10 NY offices), and (3) a general bar on fraudulent, deceitful, or misleading advertising, with fines/suspension/revocation as discipline.
- **Source:** https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/BNK/A12-D · https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/3-NYCRR-38.2 · https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry_guidance/industry_letters/il19980827_mb_advertising (DFS's own 1998 industry letter — confirm no superseding letter exists) · https://www.dfs.ny.gov/apps_and_licensing/mortgage_companies
- **NEEDS LEGAL REVIEW (flagged, load-bearing — directly affects the required license legend text):** The codified legend in §38.2 as found still literally reads "NYS Banking Department," even though that agency merged into DFS in 2011. It is unconfirmed from a primary DFS source whether (a) the regulation text has been updated, (b) DFS guidance treats "words to like effect" as covering the DFS name, or (c) the old phrasing is still literally required. **Do not let any future ad copy the "NYS Banking Department" legend verbatim without confirming current DFS guidance first** — this is exactly the kind of stale-citation risk the brief exists to prevent.
- **Also flagged:** NY Banking Law was reportedly amended by Ch. 566 of the Laws of 2024 (DFS information-sharing requirements with licensed mortgage lenders) — full text and any advertising-rule implications not verified in this pass.
